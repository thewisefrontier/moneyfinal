How to read a tarot card

Here we use the Major Arcana, the 22 core cards of tarot. They run from The Fool (0) to The World (21) and capture the big flow of a life. A card that appears upright is read as upright, and a card that appears upside down is read as reversed. A reversed card is not a bad sign; take it as a signal that the same theme is blocked or turned inward.

Open-ended questions work best, such as 'What should I remember at work this month?' Rather than redrawing again and again on the same question, take time to reflect on the first card.

Where do the card images come from?

The card faces are from the Rider-Waite-Smith deck painted by Pamela Colman Smith in 1909, a public-domain work whose copyright has expired.

Frequently asked questions

Is the card really random?

Your browser’s random number generator picks one of the 22 cards, with upright and reversed each at a 50% chance. No server decides the result.

Does the card change if I change the category?

If you change the category you need to draw again, and even the same card is read differently depending on the category.
